# CAPM-Analysis-Excel

## Project Overview 

An Excel-based Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM) analysis to examine the relationship of systematic risk and expected return across six publicly traded companies. 

## Companies Analyzed

- Procter & Gamble (PG)
- Apple (APPL)
- JPMorgan Chase (JPM)
- Exxon Mobil (XOM)
- Johnson & Johnson (JNJ)
- Coca-Cola (KO)

## Methodology

- Analyzed 60 months of historical market data from August 2021 through July 2026
- Used monthly adjusted closing prices from Yahoo Finance
- Calculated monthly returns
- Estimated beta using monthly company returns relative to the S&P 500
- Calculated the historical annualized market return
- Applied CAPM to estimate expected returns
- Compared historical returns with CAPM expected returns
- Calculated alpha
- Analyzed the Security Market Line
- Documented limitations and differences between calculated and published beta estimates

## Workbook Structure

The Excel workbook includes:

- **Raw Data** - historical adjusted closing prices
- **Returns** - monthly return calculations
- **Beta** - beta calculations and interpretations
- **CAPM** - expected returns and alpha
- **Charts** - visual analysis
- **Insights** - key findings and interpretations

## Key Findings

- P&G had the highest calculated beta at 1.09, which indicates greatest sensitivity to overall market movements.
- Coca-Cola had the lowest calculate beta at 0.18, which indicates lowest sensitivity to overall market movements.
- JPMorgan Chase had the largest positive alpha among the companies analyzed.
- Johnson & Johnson had the largest negative alpha.
- The analysis demonstrates a positive relationship between beta and CAPM expected return.
- Historical returns differed substantially from CAPM-implied expected returns for several companies.

## Limitations

- CAPM provides a simplified framework for estimating expected returns based on systematic risk. The analysis relies on historical returns and assumes the beta and the market risk premium provide and appropriate representation of future risk and return. Beta estimates can also vary depending on the time period, data frequency, and calculation methodology used.

## Conclusion

The analysis demonstrates how CAPM can be used to evaluate the relationship between systematic risk and expected return. Across the six companies, calculated beta showed differences in market sensitivity, while comparisons between CAPM expected returns and historical returns highlighted differences in risk-adjusted performance. Overall, the project shows the use of Excel for financial modeling, quantitative analysis, and communicating investment insights.
